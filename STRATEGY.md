## Judge criteria (in order — the first difference decides)

1. IT RELAXES: reading it slows the judge down and steadies it.
2. THE COURAGE COMES FROM THE TEXT ITSELF: it SHOWS rather than instructs,
   nothing feels forced.
3. CRAFT: rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete, gentle
   words, none that could be cut.
4. IT LINGERS: an image or thought you can return to that keeps working.
5. FRESHNESS: its own voice and images, no stock wellness phrases, no familiar
   meditation script reworded.

## Candidates (drafted and scored in the build session)

Four fresh images, all 148–164 chars, zero stock wellness phrases:

- A — old dog in a patch of sun (164): "On the rug, the old dog sleeps in
  a patch of sun. His breathing slows, slows, until the whole room keeps
  his time. Nothing is asked of him. Nothing is asked of you."
  Strong on 1 and 3, but the direct "you" line risks criterion 2 — it
  reassures the reader instead of letting the image do it.
- B — tide (156): "The tide comes in, goes out, comes in again. It does
  not hurry, and it does not stay away. All day it does the same slow
  work, and the shore is still there."
  Purest showing (criterion 2), wave-rhythm craft (criterion 3), but the
  "brave" note stays implicit — steadiness without a forward spark.
- C — evening light (148): "Evening light moves an inch at a time across
  the floor, and the dust in it drifts down like snow. There is nowhere
  it has to be. It is already here."
  Good pacing, but the dust-like-snow simile mixes two images and the
  ending is quieter than D's.
- D — lighthouse (149, CHOSEN): "The lighthouse turns, unhurried, all
  night long. Its light goes out across the water and comes back, and
  goes out again. Nothing is lost in the dark."
  Wins on criterion 4: "Nothing is lost in the dark" is the one line a
  reader returns to, and it delivers the competition's ask (bravery)
  without ever instructing. The turning light carries criterion 1, the
  go-out/come-back rhythm carries criterion 3, the image is fresh in
  this field (criterion 5). No address, no imperative, no stock phrase.

## Edge

One image, one rhythm, one closing thought — against a field where the
leaders are all single-image show-don't-tell texts and the instructing
texts score half as much. The entry's bet is the last line: it names the
fear (the dark) only to say nothing is lost in it.

## Tried and dropped

- Direct address to the judge ("Nothing is asked of you"): cut — it
  reassures instead of showing.
- Combining two fresh images (tide + lighthouse): cut — the winners use
  exactly one image; two dilute the rhythm.
- Any imperative ("breathe", "slow down", "you are safe"): never written —
  the field proves instructing loses by ~2x.
